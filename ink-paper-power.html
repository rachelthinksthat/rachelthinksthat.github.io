The brief

Ink, Paper, Power was our MA class's free online conference on how independent presses operate within a homogenized industry. I was assigned as marketing scheduler, and when our department was left without a lead about a month in, I stepped up to run the campaign.

Marketing's goal was not only to relay information, but integrate the style, tone, and intention of the event across each avenue we were pursuing.

What I did

  • Wrote the campaign proposal, built around a clear audience and a schedule of specific, time-bound goals for each department.
  • Researched where we could reach the most students and publishing professionals, which pointed us to Instagram, LinkedIn, Reddit and industry newsletters.
  • Wrote a brief for each team, from newsletter blurbs to a 15 second speaker snippet.
  • Worked with our speaker team to ask each speaker to share a snippet of their talk and post about the event on their own platforms.
  • Planned outreach to The Bookseller, Publishers Weekly, BookMachine and the Society of Young Publishers.
  • Kept our schedule, content bank and timeline in one shared spreadsheet, and organized our folder by task so everyone could find and distribute materials.
  • Acted as liaison between our department, design and the editors when no one took charge of meeting the schedule.

How it worked

  • Meet the team reels. We wanted the content to be human forward, so the campaign led with meet the team reels. Instagram finished with 39,350 views and 8,527 accounts reached between 27 February and 22 April.
  • Reddit. We were banned from reddit within an hour, so I posted from my personal account instead. That r/publishing post reached around 4,000 views.
  • Newsletters. My classmate's outreach landed a listing in BookMachine's newsletter.
  • LinkedIn. Followers on the MA Publishing page rose 245.6% on the previous year, to 197.
  • London Book Fair. Our tutor pointed out the chance to promote at the London Book Fair, a gap in my initial proposal, so with design and other groups we quickly produced bookmarks and handed them out.
  • Print. Design's hot pinks and grungier posters carried the independent publishing tone offline, with little promotion materials dropped around London.

The posts

A selection of what went out on Instagram and LinkedIn. Designed by the IPP design team, scheduled through the marketing plan.

Results

We measured success by Eventbrite sign ups: 161 against a goal of 200, more than previous groups.

At the end of the campaign, our Social Media Manager and I reviewed the metrics on every platform to see what had actually worked. Instagram and LinkedIn both saw a major increase in followers and connections, and post engagement rose gradually over the three months we ran the campaign.

Looking back, in proofreader's marks

stet keep it

In hindsight, having specific, actionable goals boosted the effectiveness of our team's outreach and made the entire campaign run smoothly by making specific problems or areas needing attention clear.

From the feedback I received from professors in class, I learned that our humanistic approach was highly praised by our viewers and made for an exciting and unique way to promote our event.

dele cut it

We reached thousands of eyes across multiple platforms, and even though it didn't necessarily translate into sign-ups, it did increase our connections and followers on the MA Publishing accounts.

Regardless of my skills, no one can do it all by themselves and can only set expectations and encourage others to follow through with their part of the project.

insert ‸ next time

Acting as shadow production manager taught me that I should be more communicative with my group about the role I am suited to and want to take on.

In the future, I should advocate for myself to be project manager from the start.
