Net Neutrality on Last Week Tonight with John Oliver

Mass Communications 2000 · Louisiana State University

Recently on Last Week Tonight with John Oliver the topic of Net Neutrality was discussed. John Oliver is the English host of Last Week Tonight with John Oliver on HBO, similar to The Daily Show with Jon Stewart; he says he has, “full creative freedom” of the show since there are no commercial interruptions1. The show takes a satirical look, which it normally does, at Net Neutrality. The Huffington Post says he has, “the trait Stewart's and Colbert's programs lack: perspective. Oliver has the advantage of seven-day analysis”2. The show has time to listen to the buzz and recent updates in order to prepare their story. They choose satire to simplify topics that may be hard to understand, in order to spread concern for pressing issues in society.

Last Week Tonight with John Oliver airs at 10 PM CT on HBO giving it a broad audience, aside from adolescents. People pay to watch HBO and be entertained, which makes it likely for them to watch a comical news show instead of a regular one. The same people watching are paying for their Internet, which makes them personally affected by Net Neutrality and likely to watch a segment about it. When Oliver uses “we” addressing the audience it makes them see the situation as it relates to them. People who watch regular news might see the story as a ridiculous interpretation of what is actually going on. However, people with a good sense of humor may see this as a call to action; especially when Oliver asks the crazy commenters on the Internet to give it all they’ve got on the FCC’s comment page3. People who may not have heard about this issue may especially be affected by his call to action, because it is a way they can let out what they are feeling about the issue.

The news stories that appear on Last Week Tonight with John Oliver have significance and should be spread to the public at large. Distributing news as entertainment intrigues people about what is happening around them, and makes stories that could have just been simple news go viral4. Every time the show makes another seemingly dull story go viral or spurs viewers to action, it receives publicity and views. The show is comical journalism that does not focus on one area of society; it is focused on whatever story consumed America the previous week5. This particular story is primarily political, and was spurred forth because of the recent actions of the FCC and cable companies, trying to make the Internet a two-tiered system.

John Oliver uses funny lines that don’t relate to his topic, and yet he still gets people to think about stories that would have otherwise been lost in the sea of boring news. He explains the problem the Internet faces from the potential loss of net neutrality using evidence from several different sources6. He describes the cable companies, FCC, activists, and corporations with a bias for the Internet remaining neutral instead of becoming two tiered. The bias leaves little room for a viewer to see the other side of the argument. Bashing those who stand for the two tiered Internet makes their argument seem ridiculous, and convinces people to go to the net neutrality side of the argument.

The Net Neutrality story on Last Week Tonight with John Oliver was a success, and even ended up crashing the FCC’s comment section. The funny story relates to the audience and helps them understand what exactly Net Neutrality is and why it should be talked about.
