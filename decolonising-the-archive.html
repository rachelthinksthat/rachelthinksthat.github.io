Decolonising the Archive

Notes From The Margin, Issue 002. Ring-bound zine, MA Publishing 2025-26.

The zine

A ring-bound zine designed and written by MA Publishing students (2025-26), exploring UAL's archive through a postcolonial lens. I was project manager, creating the project timeline and coordinating communication between group members, and I wrote one of the student commentaries, on the Jesuit Mission Press.

I elected to be project manager of this project so that I could establish clear roles and tasks rather than letting it all fall onto me.

My commentary

Jesuit Mission Press: Erasure Through Weaponized Religion. Opening paragraph.

The Jesuit missionaries corrupted various Indigenous American languages, believing the self righteous notion that Indigenous people needed to behave like them to be saved by Christ. They condensed and twisted the original spiritual languages, fitting them into rigid European styles of texts, using sounds from each tribe’s language with European overtones. The words Jesuits created shaved off what they deemed as excess and stripped the original meanings out of their words. The languages served pointedly as propaganda for Christianity within the Indigenous American communities and for the purposes of indoctrinating Indigenous American children with European principles and tradition. Jesuit Mission Presses documents the creation, distribution, and use of these written languages in the Pacific Northwest.
