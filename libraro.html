London Book Fair · dissertation

How a coffee line changed my dissertation

From a sigh in a queue to a dissertation case study of Libraro.

Networking

My efforts of professional development have revealed to me that the publishing industry thrives on word of mouth and community connections. Since I may not look particularly appealing through the informatics on my CV, I decided I should use my social skills to create a network of personal contacts who can vouch for my capabilities.

I considered what our tutor has told us in class about how the American way of networking is not the English way of networking, and approached networking through a different approach than I initially would have. Instead of boldly introducing myself and leading with what I needed, I needed to be a bit more nuanced in my interactions.

The coffee line

The London Book Fair just happened to be coming up, so I made it a personal goal to find connections who had knowledge about IP. Making a business connection takes a certain amount of charisma and the ability to show someone you're interested in them for more than what they can do for you. Sometimes being nonchalant is the best way to do that.

I was in the line for a coffee stand at LBF and sighing dramatically when the person in front of me asked, "everything okay with you" to which I responded, "oh yeah I'm fine, I'm just exhausted from yappin' everyone's ear off," and they laughed, which broke the ice. The conversation flowed until I naturally found my way to mentioning my interest in learning more about intellectual property.

Funnily enough the man I was speaking to is CEO of a company called Libraro that encodes IP using blockchain technology. He bought me a diet coke and we ended up speaking for thirty minutes or so about his company and my research.

The follow-up

Approaching through conversational means rather than business led me to an excellent opportunity, but in order to secure that opportunity I connected with him on LinkedIn and reached out through a DM a few days after the fair. He didn't respond at first, so I messaged again a month later. I did hear back from him after following up, and this small interaction inevitably led to me changing my dissertation topic and developing a relationship with this company to use as a point of data research.

I've learned that the most beneficial technique I can employ for myself is building rapport and making small connections with as many people as I can.
