Project Manager · art book

Embodied

Hardcover clothbound art book, MA Collaboration unit. Initial print run of six editions.

The book

Embodied is a hardcover, clothbound art book on how sculpture has represented the female figure, made by an MA Publishing group at the University of the Arts London. As project manager, I was in charge of scheduling and of communication between the editing, design and binding roles, and I kept contributor records and consent forms.

Embodied is an art book that looks at the female figure through sculptures across different forms and perspectives across time. Using images paired with commentary from artists and enthusiasts, we give the reader a space to reflect on how perceptions of the female body fluctuate and change across cultures and periods. Our commentaries were sourced from cold emails to artists, curators, and universities, along with an open submissions call to fellow UAL students and internal submissions.

Organizing the project

My main priority was establishing strong lines of communication between each department, so everyone stayed on the same page and progress was smooth. Every effective project has a strong categorization system to hold materials and communicate ideas, so that was my first priority as project manager. I chose Google Drive because everyone had access through their Gmail and the program worked well for digital collaboration.

After we had our topic approved, I created a more detailed plan in the form of a shared Google Calendar, so I could assign tasks and track deadlines. We had a shared document where we worked together to write our outreach email for collaborators, tables that held information about who was contacted, who responded, who agreed to be a contributor, consent forms once they were collected, and the commentaries they sent in.

In the end, only I used the Google Calendar for reminders. Pulling from the principles of agile project management, I exercised flexibility, and instead of forcing my group mates to adhere to the structured schedule, I adapted it as a personal management tool to maintain and monitor group progress instead, nudging the creatives as needed when approaching hard deadlines.

Adapting

Our art director, Kanna, came up with the beautiful idea of having the book itself act as a representation of the woman by having more concrete pieces on the outer pages, then moving increasingly into the abstract as you traveled toward the center. Luckily, our design manager Ilana effectively communicated that the pieces we had would look too repetitive if we went with our initial concept. As a result, we decided to pivot and go with a design that brought the movement from concrete to abstract into each spread instead of the book as a whole.

Another complication we faced was a contributor having an issue with one of the edits that had been made to her commentary. She had used specific language to refer to femininity that accurately represented her intention. We quickly fixed the edits and reassured her that we would keep the commentary as she originally had intended.

Reflection

Organization and maintaining deadlines stand out to me as my primary strengths during this project along with being the group support system. I found sending out cold emails to a variety of individuals and institutions beneficial in raising my confidence when speaking professionally within the field of publishing.

A weakness I exhibited as project manager was being too inactive. I should have set a clear scope and objectives to guide our decision-making, so that we could have been more effective in our search for sculptures. My laissez faire project management style, and trust in my team's autonomy, should not have kept me from being active in my leadership.

Art Director: Kanna. Editors: Sofia and Mila. Production, design and budget: Ilana. Binding: Ilana and Sofia.
