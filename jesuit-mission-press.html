Published commentary · UAL · 2026

Jesuit Mission Press: Erasure Through Weaponized Religion

Published in Notes From The Margin, Issue 002, a zine from MA Publishing at the University of the Arts London.

The commentary

The Jesuit missionaries corrupted various Indigenous American languages, believing the self righteous notion that Indigenous people needed to behave like them to be saved by Christ. They condensed and twisted the original spiritual languages, fitting them into rigid European styles of texts, using sounds from each tribe’s language with European overtones. The words Jesuits created shaved off what they deemed as excess and stripped the original meanings out of their words. The languages served pointedly as propaganda for Christianity within the Indigenous American communities and for the purposes of indoctrinating Indigenous American children with European principles and tradition. Jesuit Mission Presses documents the creation, distribution, and use of these written languages in the Pacific Northwest.

Initially, missionaries forced the hands of chieftains to send their children to Jesuit boarding schools for a European education by exploiting tribal worries about starvation and ruin. Tribes never saw any form of the protection or security they were promised for sending their children. Many tribes began to rally against sending their children once they realized the schools were stripping them of their cultural identity, but any concerns tribes had about their lands and traditions being taken over by these boarding schools were written off by the federal government as natural restlessness. The boarding schools were justified as necessary, allowing for the Jesuit and Sacred Heart missions to continue.

Once inside, the Indigenous children were the ones forced to spread the reduced written form of their language by working in print production, setting type for devotionals, catechisms, prayer books, and hymnals in the languages that claimed to educate but in actuality completely disrespected and erased countless tribal histories. Not only were these children isolated from their families and taught to conform to European customs, but they were also punished for expressing any form of their tribe’s heritage. Their hair was cut, language altered, clothes uniformed, and traditions overwritten with the customs and culture of the colonizers.

The Jesuits continued to fill their schools despite backlash and systematically stripped Indigenous Americans of their parental rights using federal law to “legally” enforce compliance. Indigenous families were left with no lawful avenue to protect their children. They forcibly assimilated specific nations, including the Oglala Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi, and the twelve distinct bands of the Colville Tribes, as well as the Yup'ik and Inupiaq in the far north across the Great Plains, Midwest, Pacific Northwest, and Alaska.

The Jesuits’ claim that they were doing God’s work is completely and undeniably untrue. As a Christian myself, I want to make it clear that God does not discriminate. He welcomes every nation without partiality. No matter where you come from, what you look like, who you are, or what you’ve done, God welcomes you. Forgiveness comes for those who believe in Christ Jesus, not through the way you behave, not through rituals and customs, but through faith alone. The Indigenous Americans were unjustly persecuted under a distorted version of the Gospel that weaponized faith to justify cultural genocide.
