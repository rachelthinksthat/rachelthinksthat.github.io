Graduate research review · UAL · October 2025

Can Bookstagrammers make a book more recognizable?

MA Publishing, University of the Arts London. Context unit, October 2025. A critical review of Obreja and Manolică (2024).

Article Summary

In the article, “Can Bookstagrammers Make a Book More Recognizable? The Case of ‘Bookstagram Tour’ in Romania,” Obreja and Manolică (2024) investigate how consumers’ interactions with social media and the digital world shape modern marketing. Specifically, they focus on how an influencer driven Bookstagram tour in Romania affects the popularity and visibility of books (Obreja and Manolică, 2024). Their research explores how the world of publishing overlaps with the business side of digital marketing. The relationship between the two, in combination with social media, is quantified to examine how modern media usage has affected people’s interaction with each other about books, their thought process in choosing what books to read, and ways businesses may use these changes to market books. They measure Instagrammers’ influence on a book’s market success using Instagram metrics surrounding a specific event in Romania, employing exploratory-descriptive research to determine the successfulness of such a marketing campaign in the short and long term. The study mainly aims to understand the new patterns arising in book marketing circles and how the digital relationship between influencers and consumers of books shapes book promotion (Obreja and Manolică, 2024).

Source Journal Review

Obreja and Manolică published their article in the Review of Economic and Business Studies (2025), an open access journal that covers a variety of economic topics and trends in economics and business administration. Articles in this journal must, “undergo a double-blind peer review by three referees” during the submission process (Review of Economic and Business Studies (REBS), 2025). The journal does not seem to be multidisciplinary in nature as the topics all intersect with business or economics, but the authors do publish a wide range of research that touches on many different fields of study including publishing and the literary world. REBS’ mission statement outlines high expectations for the articles they receive, requiring submissions to be “well-informed papers on the state of knowledge and supported by analysis of real facts,” or revolving around popular or contentious topics concerning politics, academics, or practices in the Economics community (Review of Economic and Business Studies (REBS), 2025). They explore broad ranges of topics with the primary goal being to untangle intertwined circles of thought in our complex world to investigate society’s relationship with business.

Research Methodology and Effectiveness

The researchers’ introduction and theoretical framework show a clear knowledge about the field of digital marketing through social media and the relationship between influencers and consumers to product visibility and commercial success. In the introduction, the authors acknowledge the limitations of their narrow scope of research in the broad book marketing realm, which displays a self-awareness about the value and boundaries of their research. The framework itself effectively communicates the key points at the core of their research and how they relate to the field as a whole by moving from a broad definition of digital marketing and the role of the influencer to the narrower scope of book influencers and their relationship with consumers, and then further specifying to the area of their data collection, the Romanian “Bookstagram Tour” (Obreja and Manolică, 2024).

Obreja and Manolică (2024) present their approach as exploratory-descriptive research seeking to better understand book promotion using bookstagrammers as a vehicle for marketing, focusing specifically on “observing and analyzing activity in the #bookstagram community during the period October 10 - October 23, 2023,” for the launch of “Too Late” by Colleen Hoover in the digital Romanian circle. The methodology section clearly outlines the parameters for data collection: influencers posted content specific to the theme of the book launch, creating a dataset for each participating creator, which was expanded on after three months. They explicitly outline the metrics collected for observation during their process, including follow counts, likes, and comments on posts during the tour.

During analysis they focus on detailed interpretation of data recorded during the promotional event even specifying down to exact findings on individual posts. Their research angle uses promotional material to quantify the influencers’ effectiveness in generating engagement. Fourteen bookstagrammers were selected by the book’s publisher to create themed posts on their feeds and stories attempting to increase the book’s online visibility, which gave the researchers a quantifiable dataset. The researchers make a note that one influencer, “individually chose not to allow these data to be viewed on Instagram” decreasing the sample size (Obreja and Manolică, 2024, p. 78), which shows their commitment to providing an accurate analysis of their findings. They also specify how data was secured, entirely online via Instagram, and identify their tools of data collection: Instagram analytics and manually collected data, with the followers being periodically checked on set dates (Obreja and Manolică, 2024).

The method clearly and carefully approaches the research question in an all-encompassing way and addresses the key areas necessary to fully understand the new developments in social media marketing as it relates to bookstagram influencers’ effect on the consumer market in a multitude of ways. Though the research is limited to a smaller sample, the concept and execution convincingly demonstrate the rising role social media marketing has in the publishing industry.

Findings Presentation

The findings presented are highly detailed in an academic style of writing. Obreja and Manolică (2024) explain the interpretation of their data and calculations clearly by using various types of tables to sort and organize their data for cleaner explanations of researched areas. They describe their process when approaching data, stating findings technically at first and then in a more accessible tone to enhance comprehension. They restate conclusions they were looking for and direct focus to the points of research used to calculate solutions. Moving through their framework systematically and explaining each point again before speaking on results highlights their understanding and improves their credibility.

Obreja and Manolică (2024) draw conclusive evidence supported findings such as table 2, “The average differences recorded during the ‘Bookstagram Tour’” (p. 72), explaining follower counts and their level of increase compared to the timeline of the book tour. All their tables are organized with clear titles and captions for each observation with further clarifications breaking down findings. Through the empirical data, the researchers definitively conclude the importance of online interaction as a method of book promotion and the effectiveness of influencer marketing.

Moving forward

Exploring online marketing through influencer culture opens many opportunities for improving book recognition and consumer reachability. The small sample sets a framework for the possibility of a study on a wider scale, though having additional elements increases the possibility of altered results that inaccurately represent data findings. Overall, the topic was well researched and will act as an effective springboard for further study.
