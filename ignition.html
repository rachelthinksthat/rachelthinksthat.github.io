Prose poem

ignition.

you wake up in the morning, open your eyes, take a deep breath, and roll yourself out of bed. you run to your closet, tear down clothes from their hangers, and mentally formulate your outfit. you move to your desk, put on each of your rings, and look at yourself in the mirror. you secure your earrings into each of your ears, then pull up your hair and fasten it. you apply your makeup with prompt precision and fluff your high ponytail. you pull on your clothes, fasten your belt into place, and slip into your shoes. you look back at the mirror, run your hands down your shirt, and turn to the side with a sigh. you readjust your hair, cock your head to the side, and purse your lips at the reflection. then you grab your purse as you rush down the stairs with your shoulders back and head high. you slide into your car, open up the garage, and put the key in the ignition.

you’re at work logging people’s hours, and receive a sudden text. your boss asks you on a date with him to a baseball game on Sunday. you look at your phone, then you look at your computer, and you put your phone face down. you keep putting in time, your neck feels hot, and you’re wondering “why would he ask me?” your boss, your boss, this is your boss, and he’s putting you in a position. he’s much older than you, you’re an intern, and you’re afraid to turn him down. you text friends asking for advice, but see none of their lazy answers. he decides your future. you text him back with a quick excuse, “i’m with my family Sundays,” then you run outside to calm down. you try to relax, but you can’t relax because you hear the door open behind you. you turn and see your boss outside, but you keep walking towards relief. he says “I got your text” and you say “yea sorry” simply trying to continue to breathe. he says “maybe next time” and you say “yeah, maybe” rushing quickly to your car. you hear his footsteps, hear the door close, and you lean against your car. tears pool around your dark eyes as you lower yourself into the car. you take a deep breath to compose yourself and you turn the key in the ignition.

you get home from work, hang your purse on a hook, and slowly take off your jacket, you shuffle up the stairs, sit down in your chair, and look at yourself in the mirror. you pick up a little rag, get rid of your face, and relieve your feet of their pain. you slide off your belt and the rest of your clothes and put on your favorite t-shirt. you take your hair down, take a look at yourself, and then slump yourself into bed. you close your eyes and take a deep breath in, and you take your key from the ignition.
