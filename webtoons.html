Graduate research paper · UAL · December 2025

The Webtoon: a master of IP jumping from platforms into multiple media formats

MA Publishing, University of the Arts London. Context unit, December 2025. 1,217 words.

The paper

Webtoons are the rising source of intellectual property for transmedia adaptations with global reach. These scrollable digital comics originate from South Korea and are hosted on platforms with a culture of audience participation. During the COVID and post-COVID era, many digital formats of media exploded in popularity, including the webtoon industry which grew about 65% and emerged as a content source for new generations (Park 2022). Korean internet platforms, like Kakao and Naver, recognized the opportunity to expand into the global content pipeline and began acquiring and merging with distribution platforms in multiple countries (Park 2022). As a result, these data-driven participatory platforms, with consumer data from multiple nations, became significant sources of adaptable IP. Platform metrics from fan participation produce verified content and limit producers’ risk when deciding acquisitions. The webtoon’s visually structured episodic storytelling streamlines the adaptation of content to different forms of media, like live-action and anime series with global success on streaming platforms. In the publishing industry as a whole, webtoons exemplify the shift towards platformization as their data-driven content rapidly converges into transmedia markets.

Distributing webtoons within a platformized environment provides producers with vast sources of data, which revolutionizes decision making for transferring content to other forms of media production. Platformization limits the need for extensive research from editors and acquisition agents by streamlining audience reception across multiple genres into quantifiable data points and connecting content likability to economic success within certain audience circles (Nieborg & Poell 2018). According to Nieborg and Poell’s research (2018) on the platformization of cultural production, gatekeeping transitions from the editor’s responsibility to the platforms through its algorithmic data recommendation systems, which reconfigures editorial labor by reducing traditional judgment calls about what content gets pushed to consumers. Webtoon platforms rely on likes, comments, subscriptions, fast-pass payments, and shares as real-time market performance indicators. Historically, publishers are faced with high risks when deciding what content will perform successfully, preferring pre-validated content with proven demand, but metrics on webtoon platforms mitigate this risk by testing IP ahead of time (Thompson 2013). Understanding audience reception gives webtoon companies the opportunity to expand globally by offering lower risk investments and highly collaborative pipelines for exporting their intellectual property (Park 2022). Publishers constantly seek out high performing content, so platform based webtoons are attractive pieces of adaptable intellectual property.

Adaptability relies on more than audience engagement based performance probability, and the addition of the storyboard serialized narrative structure of webtoons increases their suitability for transmedia production. Many webtoons publish on a weekly basis, employing TV series story structures like cliffhangers to drive engagement in a vertical scroll format, moving viewers through the images like a storyboard coming to life. A serialized structure within a participatory culture creates an opportunity for consumers’ lives, relationships, memories, fantasies, and desires to flow across media channels once the webtoon has been adapted across multiple media platforms, further enriching the reader experience (Jenkins 2006). The relationship between webtoons and their transmedia adaptations goes both ways, for example, Korea’s streaming media produces adapted webtoon shows like Hellbound and All of Us are Dead whose massive popularity on Netflix “led many viewers to seek out the original webtoon source material” (Park 2022, p. 48). Webtoons are developed with an inherent expectation of being translated across multiple mediums like video games, printed copies of webtoons, and screen adaptations. Embedding this expectation in the content itself creates a rich source of IP with a high likelihood of success across multiple platforms.

Webtoons are made for digital consumption, easily allowing creators to explore stories and take risks that traditional publishing would not, thereby amassing a variety of fresh transferable ideas with the added bonus of pre-validation from fan participation (Fang 2024). The anime adaptation of Solo Leveling gives actual evidence of the success webtoons have as transmedia in the global pipeline. The story began as a web novel that was adapted into a webtoon and picked up by A-1 Pictures to be transformed into an anime. Kakao Entertainment, the global distributor for Solo Leveling, stated the anime boasted a massive success, sweeping up several awards and ranking #1 on the anime streaming service Crunchyroll worldwide, and has been renewed for its third season (Sudario 2025). Solo Leveling’s success enacts the convergence model outlined in Jenkins’s (2006) article on convergence culture, acting as a platform-tested story that migrated across industries based on Kakao Entertainment’s corporate strategies with an overwhelmingly positive fan response on webtoon and streaming platforms alike. The seamless cross-platform performance and successful audience transfer is seen through the number of fans of the webtoon that became fans of the show and vice versa.

As the publishing industry moves forward, the role of intellectual property acquisitions and adaptations is visibly shifting from print-first to platform-first sourcing. Research from Shim et al. (2020) discusses trust within webtoon and webnovel communities acting as a form of cultural intermediation, revealing how the unique trust based relationship between creators and actively participating fans functions as the determining factor in visibility and monetary value of webtoons as intellectual property. Park (2022) looks at how, in response to a rising interest in Korean media during COVID, Korean internet platforms created digital media conglomerates to capitalize on the unique atmosphere of webtoon platforms and push fan-validated content into the global pipeline. Lamerichs (2020) builds on Park’s ideas by asserting that platforms “enable a data-driven and economic participatory culture between comic creators and their fans” (p. 213), lowering acquisition risks for transmedia adaptations. Parnell’s (2021) research offers evidence of this digital first shift as she discusses big name publishers like Penguin Random House using Wattpad’s digital platform for IP acquisitions because Wattpad offers “the extensive data that the platform gathers on users and popular content, which extend the metrics that indicate potential success” (p. 525). With a wealth of analytical information from a massive community of active fans providing real-time feedback, webtoons revolutionize IP acquisitions and incentivize publishers to seek content from digital platforms rather than taking an added risk with traditional print sourced routes.

In conclusion, webtoon popularity in transmedia adaptations makes it clear that fans have become a driving force of IP acquisitions through their engagement on digital platforms. Platformization creates current measurable data about trends in content demand through algorithm-informed metrics that reveal fan interaction volumes and receptivity on platforms hosting webtoons and other forms of digital media. Digital interfaces create visibility through a trust based circulation of stories that allow for fans to create centralized communities around beloved content. Webtoons also allow for experimental storytelling with limited risk and hook fans with serialized episodes in visually transferable mediums made for the digital platform and offering fresh ideas for transmedia adaptations. Solo Leveling’s success promotes webtoons as a desirable source for intellectual property by showing how massive fan bases will follow cherished content across multiple mediums. All in all, webtoons provide opportunity for global expansion through low risk investments and export ready intellectual property with ready made highly collaborative pipelines. Webtoons make it easy to find fan favorites in a low risk low cost portal with a quick turnover rate. In the future, publishing may rely on digital sources for IP because of the vast opportunities platforms provide, but may face challenges with algorithms controlling visibility and limiting diversity or whether they are truly representing user input.
